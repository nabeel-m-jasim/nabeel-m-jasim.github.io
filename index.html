Condensed Matter Physicist

Nabeel M. Jasim is an emerging condensed matter physicist and one of the youngest researchers to co-author a publication in Physical Review Letters (PRL), the flagship journal of the American Physical Society and one of the most prestigious physics journals in the world. PRL has published many landmark discoveries recognized by Nobel Prizes in Physics and remains one of the leading venues for transformative research in fundamental physics. His research focuses on quantum materials, charge density waves, Fermi surface reconstruction, and strain-controlled topological states.

His research combines experimental techniques including SQUID magnetometry, materials characterization, and optical instrumentation with first-principles computational methods to understand and engineer quantum states in materials.

Research Profile & Journey

Nabeel M. Jasim is a quantum materials researcher working at the intersection of first-principles Density Functional Theory (DFT) and extreme-environment condensed matter physics. His work spans prestigious international research centers across Europe and the Middle East, focusing on controlling electronic band topology, charge density waves (CDWs), and Fermi surface geometry in complex solids.

Key Appointments & International Experience

  • Center for Quantum and Topological Systems (CQTS), NYU Abu Dhabi (Jun 2026 – Jul 2026)
    Undergraduate Research Intern — Conducted first-principles DFT simulations on $c$-axis strain engineering in topological semimetals ($\text{SrAl}_4$) alongside Center Director Prof. Hisham Sati. Awarded Best Poster Award (2026).
  • Kirchhoff-Institut für Physik (KIP), Heidelberg University, Germany (Jun 2025 – Aug 2025)
    Visiting Research Fellow — Investigated phase transitions in 2D/3D charge density wave materials using ultra-low temperature, high-field SQUID magnetometry (1.8 K, 7 T) under Prof. Rüdiger Klingeler and Prof. Mahmoud Abdel-Hafiez.
  • High-Level Theoretical & Experimental Collaborations
    Engaged in advanced electronic structure frameworks in collaboration with Prof. Olle Eriksson (Chair of the Nobel Committee for Physics) and magneto-optical MOF nanostructure research with Prof. Hafsa Khurshid (Penn State University).

Computational Quantum Physics

First-principles Density Functional Theory (Quantum ESPRESSO), scalar/fully relativistic spin-orbit coupling (SOC), 3D Fermi surface reconstruction, orbital-projected DOS, and non-chemical strain engineering.

Extreme-Environment Magnetotransport

SQUID magnetometry (MPMS up to 7 T / 1.8 K), low-temperature transport (77 K / 4.2 K), AC/DC susceptibility, and Hall effect measurements under external magnetic and mechanical stress.

Spectroscopy & Nanomaterials

Advanced structural and electronic characterization: SEM-EDS, XRD, XPS, Raman, AFM, FTIR, DLS, and optically detected magnetic resonance (ODMR) for quantum sensing.

Academic Leadership: Founder & President of The Noble Society, an undergraduate research initiative dedicated to training early-stage physicists in hands-on experimental techniques, quantum control setups, and peer-reviewed scientific publishing.
