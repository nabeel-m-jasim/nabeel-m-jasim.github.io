Condensed Matter Physicist

Nabeel M. Jasim is an emerging condensed matter physicist and one of the youngest researchers to co-author a publication in Physical Review Letters (PRL), the flagship journal of the American Physical Society, which has published many landmark discoveries recognized by Nobel Prizes in Physics and remains the gold standard for transformative research in fundamental physics. Nabeel’s work focuses on the leading edge of condensed matter, specifically charge density waves (CDWs), 3D Fermi surface reconstruction, and strain-controlled topological states.

Bridging first-principles theory with cutting-edge experimental condensed matter, his international research trajectory spans premier global institutions, including the Kirchhoff-Institut für Physik (KIP) at Heidelberg University and the Center for Quantum and Topological Systems (CQTS) at NYU Abu Dhabi, where, as an undergraduate researcher, he uncovered novel strain-driven electronic topological transitions in SrAl4 that revolutionize how quantum materials are manipulated without chemical disorder. Uniquely positioning himself at the intersection of computational physics, extreme-environment transport, and nanomaterial synthesis, Nabeel combines high-level Density Functional Theory (DFT) simulations and ultra-low-temperature SQUID magnetometry with expert-level synthesis of functional nanomaterials, ranging from core-shell MOF heterostructures to engineered quantum dots. Through precision physical and structural characterization (XRD, XPS, SEM, AFM, and optical spectroscopy), he demonstrates world-class capability in controlling non-trivial electronic phases and engineering clean quantum transport channels.
